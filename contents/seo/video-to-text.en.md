## Works with video files and audio files alike

Any video with a soundtrack works — MP4 from a phone or downloaded clip, MOV from an iPhone or QuickTime, MKV, AVI, or a WebM screen recording. The tool only needs the audio track, so it doesn't matter what resolution the video is shot at or how the footage looks; a shaky phone video and a clean studio recording get read the same way.

You can also drop in an audio file directly — an MP3 voice memo, a podcast export, or any other audio recording — without needing a video container around it first. If a file has no audio track at all, the tool tells you rather than returning an empty result.

## Timestamps, copying and downloading your transcript

By default you get a clean block of text with no timestamps — good for pasting into a document or search. Turn on "Show timestamps" and the same transcript splits into timed lines instead, so you can jump straight to the part of the video where something was actually said.

Either version can be copied straight to your clipboard or downloaded as a plain .txt file. One thing worth knowing: the download is plain text, not a subtitle file — if you're specifically after an SRT or VTT file to sync captions onto the video itself, that's a different job than this tool does today, and a dedicated subtitle editor is the better fit for burning captions into a video file.

## Getting a better transcript

Speech-recognition accuracy depends heavily on what's actually in the audio, not just on the video itself:

<ul>
<li><strong>Clear, close-mic'd speech works best.</strong> A recorded call or a video with a lav mic transcribes far more cleanly than a phone recording from across a room.</li>
<li><strong>One speaker at a time helps.</strong> Heavy cross-talk or overlapping speakers is the hardest case for any transcription model, human or automated.</li>
<li><strong>Background noise and music reduce accuracy.</strong> A quiet meeting recording will transcribe better than the same conversation with music playing underneath.</li>
<li><strong>Long videos just take longer.</strong> There's no length cutoff built into the tool, but a two-hour recording naturally takes longer to process than a two-minute clip — keep the tab open and active while it works.</li>
</ul>

<p>No automated transcript is going to be word-perfect on difficult audio, and this one is no exception — treat the result as a strong first draft you can skim and correct, rather than a final copy-ready document for anything high-stakes like a legal or medical recording.</p>
