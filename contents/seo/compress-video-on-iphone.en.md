## Why iPhone videos take up so much space

<p>iPhones record in high-resolution HEVC — great for quality, brutal for file size. A minute of 4K video can easily run past 300MB, and a few minutes of slow-motion or Cinematic mode footage gets even bigger. That's fine until you try to send it: iMessage struggles with anything too large, email attachments bounce back over Gmail's roughly 25MB cap, and every video you keep chips away at your iCloud storage.</p>

<p>Compressing before you share is often the difference between a video that sends instantly and one that spins forever or fails outright. It also matters for storage: large videos are usually the biggest thing filling up an iPhone, and Live Photos and duplicate copies from AirDrop or messaging apps quietly add to the pile. SquishyFile works right inside Safari on your iPhone, so there's no app to download from the App Store — just open the page, pick a video from Photos, and squish it down to whatever size you actually need.</p>

## Tips to keep iPhone videos small from the start

<p>A little prevention goes a long way before you even reach for a compressor. In <strong>Settings &gt; Camera &gt; Formats</strong>, choosing "High Efficiency" instead of "Most Compatible" records in HEVC, which is already roughly half the file size of the older H.264 format at the same quality. And in <strong>Settings &gt; Camera &gt; Record Video</strong>, dropping from 4K 60fps down to 1080p 30fps for everyday clips — save 4K for footage you actually plan to edit or zoom into — can cut file sizes by more than half before you've compressed anything at all.</p>

<p>Cinematic mode and ProRes are worth turning off for casual recording too, since both are built for editing flexibility rather than small file sizes and can produce videos many times larger than a standard recording of the same length. None of this replaces compressing before you share — a five-minute 1080p clip can still be too big for email or Discord — but starting from a smaller file means SquishyFile has less work to do, and you keep more visible quality at every compression level.</p>

<p>It's also worth a quick pass through <strong>Photos &gt; Albums &gt; Utilities &gt; Duplicates</strong> every so often — iOS finds videos saved more than once (a common side effect of AirDropping or re-saving from Messages) and lets you merge them, which frees up space without compressing anything at all.</p>

<p>If storage still feels tight after that, it's worth checking <strong>Settings &gt; [your name] &gt; iCloud &gt; Photos</strong> — "Optimize iPhone Storage" keeps space-saving versions on your device and the full originals in iCloud, while "Download and Keep Originals" keeps every full-size file on your phone permanently. A lot of "my phone is full" moments trace back to this setting rather than to any individual video.</p>
